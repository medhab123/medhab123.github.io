About

I'm a Computer Science undergraduate at UC Irvine (Class of 2027), on the Dean's Honor List every quarter, with a cumulative GPA of 3.76. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, developer tooling, and applied statistics — I like problems where the right evaluation methodology matters as much as the model itself.

Most recently, my independent research has focused on predicting how coding agents behave from real developer–AI interaction histories, and on stress‑testing the security of agentic coding systems under adversarial prompts. Earlier work took me through epidemiological modeling, GPU‑accelerated multi‑omics pipelines, and production software at two Bay Area engineering teams.

I care about rigor over hype: repository‑held‑out evaluation splits, honest metrics under class imbalance, and reusable abstractions instead of one‑off scripts.

2026 — Presentresearch

Predicting Coding Agent Behavior from Developer–AI Interaction Histories

Independent Research · Remote

  • Formulated next‑action prediction as a 6‑class classification problem over 8,404 developer–agent turns spanning 3,191 session logs and 251 public GitHub repositories.
  • Designed a 5‑fold, repository‑held‑out cross‑validation protocol (0 shared repos between train/test) to isolate genuine generalization from spurious repo‑specific vocabulary.
  • Achieved 0.364 Macro‑F1 with multi‑turn context vs. 0.341 prompt‑only and 0.320 for a keyword‑rule baseline, a gain positive across all 5 folds (paired t‑test, p = 0.0005).
  • Diagnosed accuracy as misleading under class imbalance and standardized on Macro‑F1 for honest cross‑class evaluation.
  • Manuscript in preparation for MSR 2027 and FORGE 2027.
